# Fault campaign: protocol 6, protocol 5 with revision 7 of the SDKs

Status: fixed on 18 November 2026, before the recorded trials of this protocol.
Its SHA-256 is in [`PROTOCOL-6.sha256`](PROTOCOL-6.sha256). The schedule records the same digest.
Do not edit this file. Record any deviation in the README.

## Why a sixth protocol

Protocols 2 to 5 ran with revision 2 of the SDKs. Revisions 3 to 7 changed the session rules and the code of the owner and the client, not the frame format: a rejection of a request other than `open` now ends the session with the request possibly executed (rule 7.6.4), an owner declines a valid request that its state does not permit with `conflict`, a declined response can name a reason, and a client names its own events with local codes such as `local.timeout`.
The release record of NCP 2 lists the fault campaign on the revision that the tag names as NOT RUN.
Protocol 6 runs every trial of protocol 5 again with revision 7.

## What changes

1. The SDKs: revision 7 of the specification, with the packages 2.0.0rc7 (Python) and 2.0.0-rc.7 (Rust), at the commit that the schedule records.
2. The harness, ported to the interfaces of revision 7 without a change to what a trial does:
   - [`harness/fault_client6.py`](harness/fault_client6.py) is `fault_client4.py` with the parts of `fault_client2.py` that it used. The client takes the contract; the errors that the SDK documents for a call are `Declined`, `Rejected`, `Ended`, and `UsageError`.
   - [`harness/fault_owner6.py`](harness/fault_owner6.py) is the handler of `fault_owner2.py` and the server of `fault_owner4.py`. Its `validate` and `execute` take an `ncp.Request`, and it declines a body with `ncp.Declined`.
   - The Rust client in [`harness/rust-client`](harness/rust-client) is ported in place, with its lockfile. Protocols 4 and 5 ran its earlier form, at the commits that their results name.
   - The view of a declined step is `declined:CODE`; revision 2 had no declined outcome for the client and reported such a step as `rejected:CODE`. The view of an ended step names its verdict as `yes`, `no`, or `unknown`.
   - The invariants count a declined step as not executed. The view `protocol_error` no longer exists: a frame that does not belong to the request ends the session with `local.protocol` and the verdict `unknown`.
   - The trial program gives each server it starts, the owner and the relay, a pipe as its standard input, which stays open until the trial stops the server. The Rust echo owner serves until its standard input closes; protocols 4 and 5 ran from a terminal, which kept it open, and a runner without one gives it a closed input.
3. The view that E7 requires for a stalled step is `ended:local.timeout:unknown` (rule 7.6.7); with revision 2 it was `ended:timeout:unknown`.
4. The order of each block is shuffled with the seed 20261118, so the trials draw new kill times, frames, and bits.

The runner is `run4.py --protocol 6`, and the analysis is `analyze4.py`.

## What is the same as protocol 5

Everything else: the bindings (Unix domain sockets and TCP), the clients (Python and Rust), the owners (Python and Rust), the relay, the session of open, six steps, and close, the scenarios and their numbers of trials (4,560 in three blocks), the execution log, the invariants, the hypotheses E1 to E8 with their criteria (with the name of the view of item 3), the rule that a hypothesis holds only if every scheduled trial ran, and what the results do not show.
The trials measure no time except the detection of a stall, so they need no controlled conditions; the computer may run on battery, and the schedule records the power source.

## Smoke runs that informed this protocol

On 18 November 2026, before this protocol was fixed, the harness of protocol 5 did not build against revision 7: the Rust client used functions and error variants that revision 7 no longer has.
After the port, a smoke run of `run4.py --protocol 6` with one trial of each of the 88 combinations of scenario, owner, binding, and client, started without a terminal, found that the Rust owner exited as soon as it had printed its address: its standard input was closed. Six trials did not run, and the other Rust owner trials ended at once.
With the pipe of item 2, a second smoke run, also without a terminal and with standard input from `/dev/null`, ran all 88 trials, and every criterion held.
These runs are not results.
