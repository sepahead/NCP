# Comparison benchmark: protocol 3

This protocol was fixed on 18 November 2026, before its timed runs. Its SHA-256 digest is in [`PROTOCOL-3.sha256`](PROTOCOL-3.sha256), and the schedule records the same digest.
Do not edit this file. Record any deviation in the README.

## 1. Question

Do the results of [protocol 2](PROTOCOL-2.md) hold for revision 7 of the NCP 2 SDKs?
And did revisions 3 to 7, which changed the code of the owner and the client, change the time of an NCP 2 call?

## 2. Why a third protocol

Protocols 1 and 2 measured the SDKs of 23 October and 2 November 2026, before revision 3. Revisions 3 to 7 added the outcome `declined` with its codes and reasons, a nonce for each opening, the binding of a nameless response to the chain of the session, and checks of the `open` response, and they changed the error handling of both clients. Each of these runs on every call or on every session.
The release record of NCP 2 lists the benchmarks on the revision that the tag names as NOT RUN.

## 3. What is the same as protocol 2

Everything that this file does not change is as in protocol 2: the computer, the workload, the 140 configurations and their arms, the number of calls, three blocks, the statistic, the validity checks, the repeat of a failed run, the conditions of section 5 of protocol 2 with the exception below, and the analysis.
The order of each block is shuffled with the seed 20261118. The runner is `run.py --protocol 3`, and the analysis is `analyze.py`.

## 4. What changes

1. **The NCP 2 SDKs.** Revision 7, at the commit that the schedule records: the Rust client and owner are the examples `bench_client` and `echo_owner` of that commit, and the Python owner is `python/tests/echo_owner.py`.
2. **The Python client.** [`harness/python/ncp2_client7.py`](harness/python/ncp2_client7.py) is `ncp2_client.py` on the interfaces of revision 7: the client takes the contract, and `spawn` returns one channel that holds the child. Each call and each check of the response are as before.
3. **The clock probe.** It is built from the crate of revision 7 of the NEST campaign, `nest-campaign/harness/ncp2-rust7`, with the same source file; the crate of protocol 2 no longer builds against the SDK.
4. **Power.** Since 3 November 2026 the computer runs mostly on battery, set to High Power. As [`../conditions.py`](../conditions.py) states for protocols fixed after that day, a run may start on battery with at least 10 percent left; the clock condition, at least 4.0 GHz before a run and 3.8 GHz after it, still holds a throttled computer back, and every run records its power source.
5. **The other arms.** gRPC, ZeroMQ, HTTP/1.1, Zenoh, NCP 1.0.1, and the raw pipe are the programs of protocol 2, with the Python packages of `requirements.txt` and the lockfiles of their harnesses, so that only the NCP 2 SDKs differ.
6. **The standard input of a server.** The runner gives each server that it starts a pipe as its standard input, which stays open until it stops the server. The Rust echo owner serves until its standard input closes; protocol 2 ran from a terminal, which kept it open, and a runner without one gives it a closed input (section 8).

## 5. Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The results of protocol 1 hold again. | H1 to H5 of protocol 1, each with its decision rule, hold on the runs of this protocol. |
| R2 | In Rust, with the same integrity check and the same kind of connection, NCP 2 is faster than gRPC and Zenoh. | As in protocol 2: 9 comparisons, in every block. |
| R3 | In Python, with the same integrity check and the same kind of connection, NCP 2 is faster than gRPC. | As in protocol 2: 6 comparisons, in every block. |

A hypothesis holds when its decision rule holds. Three blocks give no confidence level.

## 6. Exploratory questions, without a hypothesis

- For each NCP 2 arm and N, the ratio of its statistic in this protocol to that in protocol 2, and whether the ranges of the block medians overlap ([`compare_revisions.py`](compare_revisions.py), which writes `revisions.md`). The two protocols ran 16 days apart, with other loads on the computer and partly on battery, so a ratio near 1 cannot separate a small change of the SDKs from a change of the conditions.
- The exploratory questions of protocol 2.

## 7. What the results do not show

As in protocol 2, section 8. In addition: the other systems are those of 2 November 2026; none of them was updated, so the comparison does not show their latest releases.

## 8. Smoke runs that informed this protocol

On 18 November 2026, before this protocol was fixed, `run.py --protocol 3 --smoke` ran without the conditions, while the 216 trials of NEST content protocol 3 kept the computer busy.
In the first smoke run, started without a terminal, the validity checks failed for the eight configurations of the Rust NCP 2 owner as a server, over a Unix domain socket and TCP: the owner had exited as soon as it printed its address, because its standard input was closed. The runner of protocol 2 passed its own standard input to each server.
With the pipe of section 4, item 6, a second smoke run passed every validity check (the tests of both SDKs, the conformance vectors, both owners against the model on 20,000 traces each, and 200,000 fuzzed frames without a difference) and ran all 140 configurations, each checking every response.
These runs are not results.
