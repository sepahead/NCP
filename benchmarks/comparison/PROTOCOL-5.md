# Comparison benchmark: protocol 5

This protocol was fixed on 19 November 2026, before its timed runs. Its SHA-256 digest is in [`PROTOCOL-5.sha256`](PROTOCOL-5.sha256), and the schedule records the same digest.
Do not edit this file. Record any deviation in the README.

## 1. Question

Do the results of [protocol 3](PROTOCOL-3.md) hold for the NCP 2 SDKs after the speed changes of 19 November 2026, and do these changes make an NCP 2 call faster?

## 2. Why a fifth protocol

The [speed bisection](speed-by-revision/README.md) found that revision 3 made one small call slower in both SDKs. On 19 November 2026, a change of both SDKs removed a part of that cost, without a change of the frame format, of a rule, or of a verdict:

- The client and the owner build the next state with its constructor, not with `dataclasses.replace` (Python), and without a copy of the outstanding request or of the old kept response (Rust).
- An owner and a client build an event only for an observer that uses it. The default observer uses only `application_error` and `server_error`.
- The Rust client copies the body of a response only for an outcome other than `ok`.
- The Python decoder parses a frame a second time only when `-0` can be a JSON number: at the start, or after `:`, `,`, or `[`. Before, a session identifier with a group that starts with `0` caused a second parse of every frame in about 4 percent of sessions.

Protocol 4 compared NCP 2 with iceoryx2. This protocol repeats protocol 3.

## 3. What is the same as protocol 3

Everything that this file does not change is as in protocol 3: the computer, the workload, the 140 configurations and their arms, the number of calls, three blocks, the statistic, the validity checks, the repeat of a failed run, the conditions, the clock probe, the Python client `harness/python/ncp2_client7.py`, the other arms, the standard input of a server, and the analysis.

## 4. What changes

1. **The NCP 2 SDKs.** Revision 7 with the speed changes, at the commit that the schedule records.
2. **The order.** Each block is shuffled with the seed 20261121. The runner is `run.py --protocol 5`, and the analysis is `analyze.py`.

## 5. Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The results of protocol 1 hold again. | As in protocol 3. |
| R2 | In Rust, with the same integrity check and the same kind of connection, NCP 2 is faster than gRPC and Zenoh. | As in protocol 3: 9 comparisons, in every block. |
| R3 | In Python, with the same integrity check and the same kind of connection, NCP 2 is faster than gRPC. | As in protocol 3: 6 comparisons, in every block. |
| S1 | The speed changes make a Python call faster. | Over the NCP 2 Python configurations with N of 0, 127, and 1788, the median of the ratio of the statistic in this protocol to that in protocol 3 is at most 0.95. |
| S2 | The speed changes do not make a Rust call slower. | Over the NCP 2 Rust configurations with N of 0, 127, and 1788, the median of the same ratio is at most 1.00. |

A hypothesis holds when its decision rule holds. Three blocks give no confidence level.
[`compare_revisions.py`](compare_revisions.py) gives the ratios: `compare_revisions.py results-3 results-5`.

## 6. Exploratory questions, without a hypothesis

- The exploratory questions of protocol 3.

## 7. What the results do not show

As in protocol 3, section 7. The two protocols ran on different days with other loads on the computer, so a ratio near 1 cannot separate a small change of the SDKs from a change of the conditions. The speed bisection, with alternating runs, measures the change of the SDKs more exactly.
