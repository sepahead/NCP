# Comparison benchmark: protocol 2

This protocol was fixed on 2 November 2026, before its timed runs. Its SHA-256 digest is in [`PROTOCOL-2.sha256`](PROTOCOL-2.sha256), and the schedule records the same digest.
Do not edit this file. Record any deviation in the README.

## 1. Question

Do the results of [protocol 1](PROTOCOL.md) hold when the computer runs on its power adapter at full speed, under controlled conditions?
And how does NCP 2 compare with the other systems when they make the same integrity check as NCP 2 and use the same kind of connection?

## 2. Why a second protocol

Protocol 1 ran on 23 October 2026 on battery power, with other programs running.
It also compares systems that give different guarantees. NCP 2 checks the integrity of every message: the sender appends the SHA-256 digest of the message, and the receiver computes it again and compares. The other arms make no such check. At 1 MiB, this check is most of the time of an NCP 2 call.
Finally, protocol 1 measures gRPC in Rust only over TCP, while NCP 2 runs over pipes, Unix domain sockets, and TCP.

## 3. What is the same as protocol 1

Everything that this file does not change is as in protocol 1: the computer, the software, the workload, the 24 arms, the number of calls, three blocks, the statistic, the validity checks of section 6, the repeat of a failed run, and the analysis of section 9 with its four figures.
The order of each block is shuffled with the seed 20261102. The runner is `run.py --protocol 2`, and the analysis is `analyze.py`.

## 4. New arms

| Arm | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| `grpc-rust-unix` | gRPC with tonic, as `grpc-rust-tcp`, over a Unix domain socket |
| `raw-rust-pipe-sha256`, `grpc-rust-unix-sha256`, `grpc-rust-tcp-sha256`, `zenoh-rust-tcp-sha256` | The Rust arms with the integrity check of NCP 2 at both ends |
| `raw-python-pipe-sha256`, `grpc-python-unix-sha256`, `grpc-python-tcp-sha256`, `zmq-python-ipc-sha256`, `zmq-python-tcp-sha256`, `http-python-tcp-sha256`, `zenoh-python-tcp-sha256` | The Python arms with the integrity check of NCP 2 at both ends |

With the integrity check (`BENCH_SHA256=1` in the environment of both ends), each sender appends the SHA-256 digest of its message, and each receiver computes the digest again, compares it, and fails the run if it differs.
The Rust arms use `sha2` 0.11.1, as the NCP 2 Rust SDK does. The Python arms use `hashlib`, as the NCP 2 Python SDK does.
The check covers the request and the response, so each call computes four digests, as an NCP 2 call does.
Each arm runs at N of 0, 127, 1788, and 87381. This gives 12 new arms, 48 new configurations, and 140 configurations in all.

## 5. Conditions

Before each run, the runner waits until all of these hold ([`../conditions.py`](../conditions.py), the rules of NEST protocol 5):

- the computer draws power from its adapter;
- its thermal pressure level is 0, nominal;
- a core runs at 4.0 GHz or more (the clock probe of the NEST campaign);
- the one-minute load average is below 8.

A run is disturbed when the computer slept for more than 0.5 s during it, or when at its end the computer ran on battery, had a thermal pressure level other than 0, or ran below 3.8 GHz.
The runner records a disturbed run and repeats it at once, at most three times in all. The analysis uses the last attempt. A run that is still disturbed after three attempts is reported and left out of the statistics.
A run that fails is repeated once, after all scheduled runs, as in protocol 1. This rule is separate from the repeats of disturbed runs.

## 6. Hypotheses

The smoke runs of section 9 suggested these hypotheses.

| # | Hypothesis | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The results of protocol 1 hold again. | H1 to H5 of protocol 1, each with its decision rule, hold on the runs of this protocol. |
| R2 | In Rust, with the same integrity check and the same kind of connection, NCP 2 is faster than gRPC and Zenoh. | At N of 0, 127, and 1788, in every block: `ncp2-rust-rust-unix` is lower than `grpc-rust-unix-sha256`, and `ncp2-rust-rust-tcp` is lower than `grpc-rust-tcp-sha256` and `zenoh-rust-tcp-sha256` (9 comparisons). |
| R3 | In Python, with the same integrity check and the same kind of connection, NCP 2 is faster than gRPC. | At N of 0, 127, and 1788, in every block: `ncp2-python-python-unix` is lower than `grpc-python-unix-sha256`, and `ncp2-python-python-tcp` is lower than `grpc-python-tcp-sha256` (6 comparisons). |

A hypothesis holds when its decision rule holds. Three blocks give no confidence level.

Exploratory questions, without a hypothesis:

- N = 87381 (1 MiB) with the same integrity check. The four digests take most of the time of every such arm, so the arms are expected to be close.
- NCP 2 against ZeroMQ, HTTP/1.1, and Zenoh in Python, with the same integrity check.
- The cost of the integrity check alone: the raw pipe with the check minus the raw pipe; and the rest of NCP 2: NCP 2 over a pipe minus the raw pipe with the check.
- gRPC over a Unix domain socket in Rust, without the check, against NCP 2 over a Unix domain socket.
- The differences between the results of protocol 1, on battery, and those of this protocol.

## 7. Analysis

`analyze.py` writes the outputs of protocol 1 and, for this protocol: the decisions of R1 to R3; tables of the new arms; a table of the cost of the integrity check alone; and a fifth figure, the call time of NCP 2 against the other arms with the integrity check, in one panel for each language and N.

## 8. What the results do not show

As in protocol 1, section 10, with one change: the arms with the integrity check give the other systems the integrity of NCP 2, but not its other guarantees. NCP 2 also gives each call an identity, executes each call at most once, says whether a call executed after a failure, and chains the responses of a session. These cost little time, but they are not in the other arms.
The integrity check of the other arms is added by the benchmark, outside each system. A system that computed the digest inside its own code could save a copy.

## 9. Smoke runs that informed this protocol

On 2 November 2026, before this protocol was fixed, short runs of 200 or 300 calls checked every new arm with and without the check, while other programs kept the one-minute load average between 4 and 8.
Every arm checked every response, and every digest matched.
In Rust, gRPC over a Unix domain socket took 45 µs at N = 0, against 81 µs over TCP, but 1311 µs at 1 MiB, against 234 µs over TCP.
With the check, the raw pipe took 972 µs in Rust and 884 µs in Python at 1 MiB, against 903 µs and 850 µs for NCP 2 over a pipe in protocol 1.
These runs are not results.
