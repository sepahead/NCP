# Comparison benchmark: protocol 4

This protocol was fixed on 18 November 2026, before its timed runs. Its SHA-256 digest is in [`PROTOCOL-4.sha256`](PROTOCOL-4.sha256), and the schedule records the same digest.
Do not edit this file. Record any deviation in the README.

## 1. Question

How does NCP 2 between two Rust processes compare with the fastest kind of local interprocess communication, request and response over zero-copy shared memory?

## 2. Why a fourth protocol

Protocols 1 to 3 compare NCP 2 with systems that, like it, move each message through the kernel: pipes, Unix domain sockets, and TCP, with gRPC, ZeroMQ, HTTP/1.1, and Zenoh. Shared memory avoids the kernel: two processes exchange a message by writing it into memory that both map, and a waiting side can poll that memory without a system call.
iceoryx2 is an open-source library of the Eclipse Foundation for this kind of communication, with request and response; its version 0.10.0 is the latest release on 18 November 2026.
NCP 2 has no binding over shared memory. This protocol measures what such a transport could save, and what NCP 2's checks cost, against the best local transport available; it does not compare guarantees.

## 3. Arms

| Arm | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| `iox2-rust-shm` | iceoryx2 0.10.0, request and response over shared memory ([`harness/rust-shm`](harness/rust-shm)): the client writes the 16-byte request into a loaned slice, the server writes the request header and N spikes into a loaned slice; both ends poll without sleeping |
| `iox2-rust-shm-sha256` | The same, with the integrity check of NCP 2 at both ends (`BENCH_SHA256=1`), with `sha2` 0.11.1, as in protocol 2 |
| `ncp2-rust-rust-pipe`, `-unix`, `-tcp` | NCP 2 revision 7 in Rust, the examples `bench_client` and `echo_owner` of the commit, as in protocol 3 |
| `raw-rust-pipe`, `raw-rust-pipe-sha256` | The raw pipe without a protocol, without and with the check, as in protocol 3 |

Each arm runs at N of 0, 127, 1788, and 87381 spikes: 28 configurations. The messages, the number of calls, the statistic (the median of three block medians, with their range), the validity checks, the repeat of a failed run, and the conditions are those of [protocol 3](PROTOCOL-3.md), battery included. The order of each block is shuffled with the seed 20261120.
The runner is `run.py --protocol 4`, and the analysis is [`analyze_shm.py`](analyze_shm.py), which uses the statistics of `analyze.py` and draws one figure.

## 4. Hypotheses

The smoke runs of section 7 suggested these hypotheses.

| # | Hypothesis | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Without an added check, iceoryx2 is faster than NCP 2 over every link. | At N of 0, 127, and 1788, in every block: `iox2-rust-shm` is lower than `ncp2-rust-rust-pipe`, `-unix`, and `-tcp` (9 comparisons). |
| Q2 | With the same integrity check at both ends, iceoryx2 is still faster than NCP 2 over a pipe. | At N of 0, 127, and 1788, in every block: `iox2-rust-shm-sha256` is lower than `ncp2-rust-rust-pipe` (3 comparisons). |

A hypothesis holds when its decision rule holds. Three blocks give no confidence level.

Exploratory questions, without a hypothesis: the times at 87381 spikes (1 MiB), where the digests take most of the time; and the difference between NCP 2 over a pipe and the raw pipe with the check, the cost of NCP 2 beyond its transport and its check.

## 5. What the results do not show

iceoryx2 gives each call none of the guarantees of NCP 2: no identity of a call, no kept response, no verdict after a failure, no chain of the responses of a session, and no check of a message unless the benchmark adds one.
Polling without sleeping keeps one core busy at each end for the whole run; a waiting side that sleeps, as the other arms do in the kernel, would take longer. The arms run one call at a time on one computer, and none of them crosses a network.

## 6. Conditions

As in protocol 3: before each run, the runner waits for the conditions of protocol 2, section 5, with battery power allowed as [`../conditions.py`](../conditions.py) states.

## 7. Smoke runs that informed this protocol

On 18 November 2026, before this protocol was fixed, short runs of iceoryx2 between two processes took 1.5 µs at N = 127 and 30 µs at 1 MiB without the check, and 2.2 µs and 848 µs with it; a server started less than a second before its client could miss the first call, so the runner starts each client only after the server has printed its name. A path as the name of the service works, so each run uses its own.
`run.py --protocol 4 --smoke`, without the conditions, while the trials of fault-campaign protocol 7 ran, passed every validity check and ran all 28 configurations: iceoryx2 took 0.83 to 1.29 µs up to N = 1788 without the check and 1.0 to 17.9 µs with it, against 9.4 to 36.0 µs for NCP 2 over a pipe or a Unix socket and 45 to 91 µs over TCP.
These runs are not results.
