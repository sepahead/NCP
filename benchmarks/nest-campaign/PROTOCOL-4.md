# NEST campaign: protocol 4

Status: fixed on 24 October 2026, before the recorded runs of this protocol.
Its SHA-256 is in [`PROTOCOL-4.sha256`](PROTOCOL-4.sha256). The schedule records the same digest.
Do not edit this file. Record any deviation in the README.

## Why a fourth protocol

Run 1 of [protocol 3](PROTOCOL-3.md) (23 October 2026) is valid for content: every NCP host received exactly the spikes that NEST recorded, and the recorded totals equal those of protocol 2.
Its timing is not reliable. The computer ran on battery for the first 30 minutes. Then its lid was closed for 7.5 minutes during block 1, which put it into a cycle of short sleeps with thermal emergency signals. Other programs raised the one-minute load average up to 125. After that, the processor stayed limited to about 3.1 GHz, against up to 4.5 GHz before, on the power adapter and with High Power selected. The `chunked` reference took as long as in protocol 2 in block 0, but 26 % and 37 % longer in the medians of blocks 1 and 2.

The cost per exchange of protocols 1 to 3 is also a difference of the wall times of two processes.
When a run has few exchanges, the variation of the network's own run time hides the cost: for 10,000 neurons at Δ = 5 ms (400 exchanges), a variation of 0.1 s in a run is 0.25 ms per exchange.

Protocol 4 therefore repeats protocol 3 under controlled conditions and adds the time of every step, measured inside the processes.

## What is the same as protocol 3

## Conditions

Before each run, the runner waits until all of these hold:

- the computer draws power from its adapter;
- its thermal pressure level is 0, nominal (`notifyutil -g com.apple.system.thermalpressurelevel`);
- a core runs at 4.0 GHz or more: the larger of two results of `harness/ncp2-rust/src/bin/clock-probe.rs`, which times eight dependent additions per loop iteration;
- the one-minute load average is below 8, half of the 16 cores.

After each run, the runner compares the time on the clock that counts sleep (`CLOCK_MONOTONIC`) with the time on the clock that does not (`CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`).
A run is disturbed when the computer slept for more than 0.5 s during it, or when at its end the computer ran on battery, had a thermal pressure level other than 0, or ran below 4.0 GHz.
The runner records a disturbed run and repeats it at once, at most three times in all.
The analysis uses the last attempt of each run. A run that is still disturbed after three attempts is reported and left out of the timing measures.

A block is admissible for timing when the median, over its configurations, of the `chunked` wall time divided by protocol 2's for the same configuration and block lies between 0.9 and 1.1.
When a block is not admissible, the timing hypotheses (G1, G2, G3, G6, G7) are not adjudicated, and the README reports why.

## Time of each step

Each arm records the duration of every step with the monotonic clock of its process. The runner stores summaries in the record of the run and deletes the raw durations.

| Duration | Arms | From | To |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work | all but `mono` and `music` | before `nest.Run` | after the readback of the count or of the new spikes |
| Call | NCP arms | before the host sends the request | after the host has verified and decoded the response |
| Round trip | `chunked-pipe` arms | after the readback | after the echo is back, the packing of the message included |

The overhead of a step is what the coupling adds to it: for an NCP arm, the host's call minus the owner's work of the same step; for a `chunked-pipe` arm, the round trip.
For NCP 1.0.1, a call includes its explicit acknowledgement.
Each summary gives the number of steps, the mean, the median, the 90th and 99th percentiles (nearest rank), and the largest value.

## Measures

The measures of protocol 3, and:

- the overhead per exchange of each NCP arm and each `chunked-pipe` arm: the median over blocks of the run's median, and of the run's 99th percentile;
- the work per step of each arm: the median over blocks of the run's median;
- exploratory: the pause effect, the work per step of a coupled arm minus that of `chunked` (or `chunked-spikes`) in the same block, as a median over blocks. It is the slowdown of NEST's own work when NEST stops for a round trip after each interval.

## Smoke runs that informed this protocol

On 24 October 2026, after the change of the harness, one block of 22 configurations ran: Brunel with 1000 neurons, 1 thread, Δ = 1 ms; 10,000 neurons, 4 threads, Δ = 1 ms; and the D4 arms with 1000 and 10,000 neurons at Δ = 5 ms.
The computer was on its adapter at thermal pressure 0, with load averages from 3.2 to 4.1, but, as found afterwards, its processor was limited to about 3.1 GHz, and `chunked` took 1.17 to 1.35 times as long as in protocol 2.
The median overhead per exchange was 13 and 14 µs for the raw round trip, 29 and 35 µs for NCP 2 with the Rust host, 63 and 67 µs with the Python host, 299 and 313 µs for NCP 1.0.1 with the Rust host, and 628 and 643 µs with the Python host.
With spikes, it was 19 and 58 µs for the raw round trip, 66 and 144 µs for NCP 2 with the Rust host, and 106 and 190 µs with the Python host.
The difference of wall times gave 23 µs for NCP 2 with the Rust host at 1000 neurons and 145 µs with spikes at 10,000 neurons, close to the overhead.
The work per step of the coupled arms differed from that of `chunked` by at most 20 µs at Δ = 1 ms.
A profile ([`profiling/`](profiling)) found that the owner's protocol code runs two to four times slower inside the NEST process than without NEST.
These results are not results of this protocol.

## Hypotheses

G1 to G5 are those of protocol 3, evaluated on the runs of this protocol; G5 compares the recorder totals with those of protocols 2 and 3.

- **G6.** D1 with Δ = 1 ms: for each size, thread count, and host language, the median overhead of NCP 2 is lower than that of NCP 1.0.1 in every block (12 comparisons).
- **G7.** D4: each of the four configurations of `ncp2-rs-spikes` has a median overhead of at most 0.15 ms, the design target of G3 applied to the overhead alone.

A hypothesis holds when its criterion holds. Three blocks give no confidence level.

## Not claimed

As in protocol 3. The overhead leaves out the pause effect, so it is not the whole cost of a coupling; G3 tests the whole cost.
